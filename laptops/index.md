<section class="prose" aria-labelledby="laptops-question-title">
  <h2 id="laptops-question-title">A recommendation should mean something.</h2>
  <p>Omarchy is exploring what it would take to stand behind a laptop recommendation. A logo on a lid would be cheap. A useful standard would have to describe the experience of living with a machine: opening it, fixing it, and using the parts that make a computer feel like a computer.</p>
  <p>That work begins with questions, not a list of machines.</p>
</section>

<section class="prose" aria-labelledby="laptops-evidence-title">
  <h2 id="laptops-evidence-title">What evidence should earn the name?</h2>
  <ul>
    <li>What does repairability look like when a person needs to understand and service their own machine?</li>
    <li>Which parts of the hardware experience need direct, repeatable testing before anyone can make a useful recommendation?</li>
    <li>What should an honest recommendation say when it cannot assume every reader can get the same machine?</li>
  </ul>
</section>

<section class="prose" aria-labelledby="laptops-standard-title">
  <h2 id="laptops-standard-title">The standard matters more than the badge.</h2>
  <p>Any future evaluation should be clear about what it observed and what it did not. That is how a recommendation stays useful when the person reading it has different needs, a different desk, or the nerve to open the back panel.</p>
  <p>For current proof of how people shape Omarchy around the hardware they already use, visit <a href="{{ '/workstations/' | relative_url }}">Workstations</a>.</p>
</section>
