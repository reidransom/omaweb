<section class="prose" aria-labelledby="cloud-question-title">
  <h2 id="cloud-question-title">A workstation somewhere else still has to feel like yours.</h2>
  <p>Omarchy is exploring what a hosted workstation would have to preserve before it deserves the name. A remote machine can change where work runs, but it should not blur who owns the environment, who can inspect it, or what happens to the work left inside it.</p>
  <p>That is a question of boundaries, not a shortcut around them.</p>
</section>

<section class="prose" aria-labelledby="cloud-continuity-title">
  <h2 id="cloud-continuity-title">What must persist when the screen changes?</h2>
  <ul>
    <li>What should remain in an environment between sessions, and what should remain only with the person who created it?</li>
    <li>How could synchronization stay understandable when files, configuration, and agent work move between machines?</li>
    <li>What kinds of compute belong in a hosted environment, and how should that choice stay visible to the person using it?</li>
  </ul>
</section>

<section class="prose" aria-labelledby="cloud-ownership-title">
  <h2 id="cloud-ownership-title">Security and ownership cannot be footnotes.</h2>
  <p>Any serious exploration has to begin with access, data boundaries, and the ability to understand what is happening to your work. Convenience is useful. Control is the point.</p>
  <p>The current, local place to shape an Omarchy environment is <a href="{{ '/desktop/' | relative_url }}">Desktop</a>.</p>
</section>
