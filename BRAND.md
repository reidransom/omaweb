# Omarchy Brand Guide

A source-derived working guide for expressing Omarchy consistently across product, web, editorial, community, and social surfaces.

This guide reflects the live public brand on [omarchy.org](https://omarchy.org/), its [Manual](https://omarchy.org/manual/), [News](https://omarchy.org/news/), and published social assets. It documents observed patterns, then turns them into practical rules. It does not grant trademark or asset-usage rights; the site states that Omarchy is a pending trademark.

## 1. Brand core

### Name

**Omarchy**

Always capitalize the initial **O**. Do not set the prose name in all caps; reserve `OMARCHY` for the display wordmark.

### Primary descriptor

> **Beautiful, Fun & Opinionated Linux by DHH**

Preserve the capitalization, ampersand, and three-part rhythm when using this line as the homepage descriptor.

### Product promise

> **The malleable OS for the age of agents.**

Omarchy presents Linux as a complete, aesthetically coherent, productive system that users can shape—directly or with an agent—rather than a generic desktop assembled from unrelated parts.

### Brand idea

**A beautiful computer is a motivating computer, and an opinionated system can still be deeply malleable.**

The brand holds several productive tensions:

- **Beautiful, not ornamental:** aesthetics serve motivation and daily use.
- **Opinionated, not closed:** strong defaults are the starting point, not a cage.
- **Technical, not sterile:** terminal fluency coexists with humor, taste, and delight.
- **Modern, not corporate:** agents, tiling, TUIs, and customization without enterprise jargon.
- **Complete, not bloated:** an integrated system with “everything I use,” not a grab bag.
- **Adventurous, not hostile:** Linux-ness is part of the appeal, while the user is invited along.

### Audience

Primary audiences:

- Developers and technically curious computer users.
- People who value keyboard-first workflows, tiling window management, terminals, and customization.
- Users who want excellent defaults without surrendering control.
- Agent-assisted builders who expect to alter their system through plain-language intent.
- Design-conscious Linux users who see aesthetics as part of productivity.

### Personality

| Trait | Expression | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Beautiful | Deliberate composition, rich imagery, coherent themes | Decoration without function |
| Fun | Energy, surprise, retro-computing references, light humor | Forced jokes or meme overload |
| Opinionated | Clear recommendations and confident defaults | Neutral “anything goes” language |
| Malleable | Emphasize control, alteration, and user agency | Presenting the system as fixed or locked down |
| Technically fluent | Precise product names and direct technical language | Explaining every basic term or hiding behind jargon |
| Human | First and second person, contractions, honest preferences | Corporate abstraction and committee voice |
| Ambitious | “Computer of the future,” agents, new ways of working | Empty superlatives without a concrete idea |

## 2. Messaging hierarchy

Use this order when space allows:

1. **Omarchy** — the name or wordmark.
2. **Beautiful, Fun & Opinionated Linux by DHH** — what it is and how it feels.
3. **The malleable OS for the age of agents.** — why it matters now.
4. A concrete action: install the ISO, read the Manual, explore Plugins, or command an agent.
5. Evidence: product imagery, creator coverage, downloads, community, or releases.

### Short description

> Omarchy is a beautiful, fun, and opinionated Linux distribution built to be productive immediately and malleable all the way down.

This is a working description derived from the site, not a replacement for the primary descriptor.

### Standard description

> Omarchy is an omakase Linux distribution based on Arch, Hyprland, and Quickshell. It combines strong defaults, terminal-first workflows, and a coherent visual system with the freedom to alter the computer to fit the user—especially through agents.

### Key themes

Use these themes repeatedly, but connect each to something concrete:

- Beauty as motivation.
- Strong defaults as leverage.
- The malleable computer.
- Agents as a new interface for system change.
- Productivity without bloat.
- Delighting in the “Linux-ness” of the system.
- A complete system, not a pile of packages.
- Community, artists, plugins, and people who shape the ecosystem.

### Naming pattern

The site often makes destinations feel definitive and tangible:

- **The Manual**
- **The ISO**
- **The Plugins**
- **The Code**
- **The News**
- **The Teams**
- **The Patrons**
- **The Workstations**

Use this pattern for major, singular destinations. Do not apply “The” mechanically to every feature or sentence.

The user identity is active and playful: **“Be the Omarch.”** Use sparingly as a high-energy call to action, not as the default name for every user.

## 3. Voice and tone

### Voice principles

#### 1. Lead with a point of view

Say what is better, more beautiful, more useful, or worth trying. Explain the reason plainly.

- **Use:** “A beautiful system is a motivating system.”
- **Avoid:** “Different users may have different aesthetic preferences.”

#### 2. Write to one capable person

Use **you**, **we**, and **I** where ownership matters. Assume curiosity and competence.

- **Use:** “Embrace the Linux-ness of it all.”
- **Avoid:** “End users are encouraged to familiarize themselves with Linux workflows.”

#### 3. Prefer concrete nouns and active verbs

Name Arch, Hyprland, Quickshell, Neovim, the terminal, the ISO, and the config file. Avoid vague platform language.

- **Use:** “Install the ISO, open the Manual, and make it yours.”
- **Avoid:** “Leverage the platform to unlock customized experiences.”

#### 4. Keep the energy human

Contractions, fragments, parenthetical asides, and the occasional exclamation point are on-brand. They should sharpen the thought, not perform excitement.

- **Use:** “Heavy on the terminal, definitely.”
- **Avoid:** “Revolutionize your workflow today!!!”

#### 5. Make bold claims legible

Pair ambition with a product truth, example, or evidence.

- **Use:** “The malleable OS for the age of agents” followed by how agents alter the system.
- **Avoid:** “The world’s most advanced operating system.”

### Cadence and mechanics

- Favor short to medium sentences, then vary rhythm with a fragment.
- Use contractions naturally: **isn’t**, **it’s**, **you’re**.
- Use sentence case for editorial headings.
- Use title case only where the existing product or destination name requires it.
- Use `&` in the primary descriptor; use **and** in normal prose.
- Use exclamation points sparingly, usually once.
- Parentheses can carry wit or a quick aside: “Neovim (btw).”
- Avoid buzzwords such as *seamless*, *best-in-class*, *unlock*, *solution*, and *ecosystem* unless the literal meaning is necessary.
- Avoid faux-rebellion, macho gatekeeping, and hostility toward newcomers.

### Tone by context

| Context | Tone |
|---|---|
| Homepage | Compressed, confident, inviting, high-energy |
| Manual | Direct, generous, technically precise, conversational |
| News | Declarative, specific, enthusiastic; people and outcomes first |
| Release notes | Factual and scannable, with restrained personality |
| Errors/security | Calm, exact, accountable; no jokes that obscure action |
| Community | Warm, participatory, celebratory without corporate cheerleading |

## 4. Logo system

### Primary wordmark

The homepage uses a custom `OMARCHY` wordmark built from block-character ASCII art. Its terminal construction is essential to the identity: technical, handmade, retro, and native to a monospaced environment.

On the live homepage, an animated canvas treatment overlays the mark with luminous cyan, blue, and purple movement. A static fallback renders the mark in **Green**. Reduced-motion mode also uses the static treatment.

**Rules**

- Preserve the supplied letterforms. Do not retype the word in an arbitrary pixel font.
- Keep the mark monospaced and horizontally proportioned.
- Use the animated treatment only where motion is supported and nonessential.
- Always provide the Green static fallback.
- Maintain strong contrast against **Night**.
- Keep the area around the mark quiet; do not place it over detailed imagery.
- Do not add outlines, bevels, glows, or gradients beyond the approved animated treatment.
- Do not stretch, skew, rotate, or crop the mark.

### Compact mark

The square social asset uses an interlocking, maze-like geometric monogram in Green on Night. Use it when the full wordmark cannot remain legible: avatar, favicon, app tile, or compact social placement.

Do not recreate the compact mark from ordinary glyphs. Use the supplied artwork.

### Static social wordmark

The Open Graph asset uses a simplified block-letter `OMARCHY` wordmark in Green on Night. It is appropriate for static previews and wide social placements, provided its copy is current.

### Clear space and minimum size

No official measurements are published on the site. Until supplied assets define them:

- Keep at least one block-stroke width of clear space around either mark.
- Use the full wordmark only when every interior counter remains visible.
- Switch to the compact mark rather than forcing the wordmark into a small square.

## 5. Color

The palette is terminal-derived and dark-first. Night supplies the canvas; Blue carries most interface content; Green identifies the static mark; Cyan and Turquoise create interactive lift.

| Token | Hex | RGB | Primary role |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Night | `#1A1B26` | `26, 27, 38` | Page background; dark text on light controls |
| Storm | `#24283B` | `36, 40, 59` | Raised surfaces and media placeholders |
| Terminal Black | `#414868` | `65, 72, 104` | Subtle borders, typically at 80% opacity |
| Terminal Blue | `#7AA2F7` | `122, 162, 247` | Primary text, buttons, announcement bars |
| Terminal Cyan | `#7DCFFF` | `125, 207, 255` | Links and secondary interactive emphasis |
| Green | `#9ECE6A` | `158, 206, 106` | Static wordmark and core identity accent |
| Turquoise | `#B4F9F8` | `180, 249, 248` | Hover state and bright interactive accent |
| Terminal White | `#C0CAF5` | `192, 202, 245` | Optional high-emphasis light neutral |
| White | `#FFFFFF` | `255, 255, 255` | Link hover and maximum contrast |
| Black | `#000000` | `0, 0, 0` | Shadows only; avoid as the main page background |

### Contrast and pairing

Measured WCAG contrast ratios on Night:

- Terminal Blue: **6.79:1**
- Terminal Cyan: **9.96:1**
- Green: **9.35:1**
- Turquoise: **14.52:1**
- Terminal White: **10.59:1**
- White: **17.09:1**

Night on Terminal Blue also measures **6.79:1**, making the observed button and announcement pairing suitable for normal text.

**Rules**

- Default to Night backgrounds; the public identity is dark-first.
- Use Terminal Blue for body copy and primary controls.
- Use Cyan for linked text and Turquoise for hover, not as competing decorative accents.
- Reserve Green for the mark or a deliberate identity cue.
- Use White sparingly; it should feel like a peak highlight.
- Do not introduce unrelated rainbow neon, warm corporate gradients, or large white canvases.
- Never communicate state by color alone.

## 6. Typography

### Identity family

**JetBrains Mono**, monospace fallback.

The monospaced voice is structural, not a novelty treatment. Use it for the wordmark, display type, navigation, buttons, labels, metadata, commands, and compact product copy. It remains the default family when a surface does not need sustained reading.

### Editorial family

**Source Serif 4**, serif fallback.

Use Source Serif 4 for sustained editorial reading: News articles, essays, interviews, long narrative passages, decks, and pull quotes. Keep surrounding navigation, metadata, commands, and controls in JetBrains Mono so the terminal construction remains explicit.

Source Serif 4 may carry an editorial headline when the entire page is reading-first. Product and destination headings stay in JetBrains Mono.

### Accessible sans family

**Atkinson Hyperlegible Next**, sans-serif fallback.

Use Atkinson Hyperlegible Next where rapid character recognition matters more than editorial tone: onboarding, setup instructions, dense guidance, accessibility-led content, and long functional explanations. Keep labels, buttons, code, shortcuts, filenames, and commands in JetBrains Mono.

Atkinson Hyperlegible Next is a functional reading face, not a replacement for the primary family across marketing pages.

### Weights and styles

JetBrains Mono:

- **300 Light:** default compact body and headline weight.
- **300 Light Italic:** hero supporting copy.
- **400 Regular:** utility copy and static ASCII mark support.
- **500 Medium:** compact metadata and footer copy.
- **700 Bold:** links, buttons, and strong emphasis.

Source Serif 4 and Atkinson Hyperlegible Next:

- **400 Regular:** default reading weight.
- **400 Italic:** editorial emphasis and quotations. Use Source Serif 4 italic where possible.
- **700 Bold:** subheads and strong emphasis.

Do not load additional weights without a defined role. Both supporting families use the SIL Open Font License 1.1. Self-host production WOFF2 files rather than fetching them from a third-party font service.

### Scale

The site uses a fluid, compact type system:

```css
--font-family: "JetBrains Mono", monospace;
--font-family-editorial: "Source Serif 4", Georgia, serif;
--font-family-readable: "Atkinson Hyperlegible Next", system-ui, sans-serif;
--font-size: clamp(0.625rem, 1.25vw, 1rem);
--font-size-small: 80%;
--font-size-medium: 125%;
--font-size-large: 200%;
--line-height: 1.4;
--line-height-reading: 1.6;
```

The homepage `h1` is only 125% of body size and Light weight. Hierarchy comes from the wordmark, spacing, alignment, and imagery, not oversized marketing type.

**Rules**

- Use JetBrains Mono plus no more than one supporting family on a page.
- Do not combine Source Serif 4 and Atkinson Hyperlegible Next in the same content hierarchy.
- Keep line length around **46em** for prose.
- Set sustained reading copy at Regular weight with approximately **1.6** line height.
- Center only hero and compact footer copy; use normal reading alignment for long editorial text.
- Use Bold for interaction and emphasis, not for whole paragraphs.
- Keep the ASCII mark’s line height tight, approximately **1.09375**.
- Do not introduce another geometric sans to make the design feel “friendlier.”

## 7. Layout and spacing

### Composition

The homepage is a centered, single-column sequence:

1. Full-width announcement bar.
2. Primary wordmark.
3. Small descriptor and italic supporting copy.
4. Wrapped navigation buttons.
5. Large hero video.
6. Two-column creator-video grid on wider screens.
7. Compact, centered provenance and partnership footer.

The effect is dense in information but calm in structure. One focal object per vertical beat.

### Spacing scale

```css
--space-small: 0.5em;
--space-medium: 1em;
--space-neutral: 1.5em;
--space-large: 2em;
--space-x-large: 3em;
--space-xx-large: 4em;
--space-xxx-large: 5em;
--space-xxxx-large: 6em;
```

### Responsive behavior

- Default horizontal padding: **2em**.
- Wide-screen padding at `64em` and above: **4em**.
- Media width: up to **90em**.
- Video grid: one column by default, two columns from `40em`.
- Mobile navigation uses one wrapping flex context so buttons distribute without stranded rows.
- Wide navigation restores deliberate grouped rows from `64em`.
- Preserve the same content hierarchy at every size; reduce scale and columns, not meaning.

## 8. Interface components

### Buttons

Observed primary button:

- Terminal Blue background.
- Night text and icon.
- Bold, uppercase label.
- Icon on the left at `1.4em`.
- `1ch` icon-to-label gap.
- `2.9em` control height.
- `1em` inline padding.
- `0.4em` corner radius.
- Turquoise hover background.
- Soft, layered black shadow.

Buttons should feel compact and tool-like—not pill-shaped, glossy, or oversized.

### Links

- Terminal Cyan, Bold.
- Visible underline with deliberate thickness and offset.
- White on hover.
- Never remove the underline from inline prose links.

### Announcement bar

A flat Terminal Blue strip with centered, bold Night text. No radius. On hover, shift the background to Turquoise. Keep the message to one concise line where possible.

### Media

- Use a **16:9** ratio.
- Use `0.4em` corners.
- Place imagery on Storm while loading.
- Keep a familiar YouTube-style play affordance when linking to video.
- Hover may scale the image to **1.02** and raise play-icon opacity.
- Do not autoplay embedded media.

### Icons

- Simple, filled or solid monochrome SVGs using `currentColor`.
- Function first: book for Manual, download for ISO, puzzle for Plugins.
- Consistent visual mass at small size.
- Avoid multicolor icon packs, emoji, or mismatched line weights.

### Motion

The global interaction timing is:

```css
--transition: 0.15s cubic-bezier(0.33, 1, 0.68, 1);
```

Motion is quick and purposeful. The logo may carry the expressive motion; controls should use restrained color, opacity, and slight scale transitions. Honor `prefers-reduced-motion` and retain a complete static experience.

## 9. Imagery and art direction

### Desired visual territory

- Saturated retro-futurism and synthwave color.
- Real Omarchy desktops, terminals, TUIs, and workstations.
- Hardware with character.
- Community creators and recognizable people using or discussing the system.
- Scenes that show beauty and utility at once.
- High-color imagery framed by the dark Night canvas.

The homepage’s lead image—a rally car in a saturated sunset environment—adds speed, attitude, and playful excess. Supporting thumbnails provide proof through people and real interfaces.

### Avoid

- Generic office stock photography.
- Abstract enterprise technology imagery.
- Empty neon grids used as decoration.
- Screenshots too small to communicate the product.
- Uniformly polished renders that erase the human, tinkered-with quality.
- AI-generated imagery that introduces illegible UI, fake terminal text, or distorted hardware.

## 10. Accessibility and product quality

Brand expression must remain functional.

- Preserve keyboard focus states on every interactive element.
- Keep inline links underlined.
- Supply useful alternative text or accessible names for images, icons, and video facades.
- Meet WCAG AA contrast for all text and essential controls.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences; the static Green wordmark is the fallback.
- Do not fetch third-party video embeds before the user activates them.
- Keep touch targets usable even when labels and type remain visually compact.
- Use semantic headings; visual restraint is not a reason to flatten document structure.

## 12. Brand checklist

Before publishing an Omarchy-branded surface, verify:

### Message

- [ ] The copy has a clear point of view.
- [ ] The value is concrete, not generic.
- [ ] Technical names and claims are accurate.
- [ ] The tone is human and direct.
- [ ] The primary descriptor is reproduced exactly when used.

### Visual

- [ ] The correct wordmark or compact mark is used without distortion.
- [ ] Night is the main canvas and the palette uses approved tokens.
- [ ] JetBrains Mono carries identity, navigation, controls, labels, metadata, and technical text.
- [ ] Source Serif 4 is limited to editorial reading, or Atkinson Hyperlegible Next is limited to accessible functional reading.
- [ ] No page uses both supporting families in the same content hierarchy.
- [ ] Hierarchy comes from composition and spacing, not giant type.
- [ ] Imagery shows real product, people, or intentional retro-futurist art.
- [ ] Buttons, links, corners, shadows, and motion match the system.

### Quality

- [ ] Text and controls meet contrast requirements.
- [ ] Focus, reduced motion, alternative text, and responsive behavior work.
- [ ] Social metadata uses current copy and approved assets.
- [ ] Trademark and partner references are accurate.
